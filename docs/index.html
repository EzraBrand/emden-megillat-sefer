About this text

Hebrew: David Kahana's edition (Warsaw 1896/97), from the HebrewBooks scan no. 37017, which is Gershom Scholem's copy (his library stamp is on scan p. 196). OCR by Kraken with the PP-OCRv6 model, then checked line by line against the scan by Claude. Running heads and page numbers are removed; Kahana's footnote calls are shown as small letters.

English: draft translation by Claude (Anthropic), not yet reviewed. Biblical phrases are given in standard wording with the reference. Kahana's own footnotes are translated under each page.

Scholem's copy: the margins carry handwritten insertions, most of them words that Kahana left out of the manuscript text (often harsh terms for non-Jews). They are shown as ⟨S: …⟩ where his caret points, and listed with their certainty under each page. A typed sheet bound in after p. 8 copies a slip of the Oxford manuscript. Some notes are cut off at the edge of the scan.

Not the manuscript: Kahana's text is not a full transcription of the Oxford manuscript (Bodleian, Neubauer 1723:2). A critical edition by Jacob J. Schacter has been announced.

My father! May the Keeper of truth guard you forever, in the light of the Lord. "Gather together and hear, O Jacob" (Gen. 49:2), and may God hear you — the God of your gods, the God of your fathers.

I will tell of the decree (Ps. 2:7) and ascribe righteousness to my Maker (Job 36:3), and I will say: "Many things have You done, O Lord my God, with me; were I to declare and speak of them, they are too many to be numbered" (Ps. 40:6) — and to engrave them in a book: "where is he who weighs, where is the scribe?" "O Lord, You are my God; I will exalt You, I will praise Your name, for You have done wonders, counsels from afar" (Isa. 25:1) [c]. The Lord has appeared to me through His deeds and the acts that He showed me against my adversaries. "Oh that my words were now written, oh that they were inscribed in a book, … that they were engraved in the rock for ever" (Job 19:23–24), in the writing of the house of Israel, so that "children yet to be born may arise and tell them to their children" (Ps. 78:6) at the last day. "Then will our mouth be filled with laughter and our tongue with song" (Ps. 126:2), for wickedness and iniquity will be no more.

From his childhood "the spirit of the Lord began to move" my father (Judg. 13:25). He wrote and composed long and broad responsa, of ten sheets of paper or more. There in Altona the Blessed One built him "a sure house" (1 Sam. 2:35), and set up for him the great house of study, the Klaus, which the magnates and rich men of the Three Communities founded for him, to spread true Torah, for its own sake. Outstanding students and rabbis from the lands of Poland and Lithuania gathered to him there, and studied Torah there with great diligence; day and night they did not cease to learn Gemara, Rashi and Tosafot, the codes, halakhot and aggadot. They also "filled their hands to the Lord" with other knowledge, until they became perfect in Torah and in wisdom. Many times they completed with him the whole Talmud and the Ṭurim, Bible and Midrash, and a book of grammar.

They also learned from him abstinence and caution, the way of true piety and inward fear of Heaven. For he hated unjust gain, and despised even money that other rabbis counted as honest. Never in his life did he take gifts of money from flesh and blood, from private persons, although many times the leaders of the people, the rich men of his generation — men great and important like princes and dukes — wanted to make him rich. Many times they brought him presents, precious vessels of silver and gold, and he sent them away empty-handed and would take nothing from them.

Who can tell the greatness of his deeds? They would not be believed if they were told — except by one who saw and knew and served him. Even now, at the time I write this scroll, there are still in the Three Communities some old men who served him and remember his deeds, his holy and upright conduct and his teaching; they are filled with joy and gladness when they speak of him. "To his name and to his memory is the desire of the soul" (Isa. 26:8) of those who knew him of old, for none like him was heard or seen in these lands since ancient years. Therefore he grew and prospered in the Torah and wisdom that he learned and taught; for all his Torah was for the sake of Heaven, not to make himself great or to find favour in the eyes of men. All his aim and desire was to increase the glory of his Creator and to raise up the pillars of the Torah, with all his heart, all his soul and all his might.

He had the heart of a lion to rescue the oppressed from the oppressor; he showed no favour in matters of Torah, and kept the command "You shall not be afraid of any man" (Deut. 1:17). Because of this trait some of the powerful "rams" of the city were always opposed to him, as I will write, with God's help — not enemies of his person, God forbid (except some worthless men, the insolent of the generation); for everyone knew and recognised that he was a man of God, holy and faithful to God. For this very reason they feared him: because he would not show them favour in anything that was not upright. There is no wonder in this. A man who would not take from a human being even if they gave him all the silver, gold and pearls in the world will surely not show partiality in judgement, and will do only what is right in the eyes of the Lord alone.

Many times my father cried out about this, and laid his words before the great men of Germany, that they should exert themselves to remove this stumbling-block and to lock the door against the evil-doers — and it would have been worthy to bring the redemption nearer. But he cried out and no one paid attention; for "there is no family that has no tax-collector in it, or [else] it is all tax-collectors" (b. Shevu'ot 39a). These extortioners have families of powerful and violent men who cover for them and strengthen their hands, so as not to "bring out of their mouth what they have swallowed" (Jer. 51:44), and who acquire the money of the poor by "pulling" it from the public domain into their own, to increase the honour of their houses. How much great money sank into the hands of those men, and the Land of Israel is left desolate for ever, until the Lord is jealous for His land and for His people, for His name's sake.

Even in the holy community of Altona, the leader R. Ber Kohen opposed him, so that this great thing should not be carried out. He rose up as an adversary to this good matter, and withheld the good, so that they should not have the merit of this great commandment. More of his godly traits: even if they had given him all the treasures of the world, he would not have gone back on his word. No word of jest or levity was ever heard from his mouth, although he was of a cheerful mind and spoke to every man with a pleasant face. With all this he was feared and held in awe, and loved and dear to all who knew him; and those who knew him held him bound(?) to strengthened holiness and purity and an extra spirit: little speech, little pleasure, little sleep (cf. Avot 6:6); full of the spirit of wisdom and fear of Heaven, discipline and humility. He did not look beyond his four cubits. "His delight was in the Torah of the Lord" all day and all night; in it he meditated, "in its love he was ever ravished" (Prov. 5:19). And though "the Torah made him hot" (b. Ta'anit 4a), still he was accepted by the people close to him, who knew his ways, because they knew that all his deeds were for the sake of Heaven, without any outward motive.

Therefore his friends and disciples devoted themselves to him: they cared for him and provided him with a livelihood with honour, so that he was not forced to leave the trait of piety that he had held from his youth, not to accept a gift. He did not enjoy an optional banquet; and they could not move him against his will to agree to accept presents — for several leaders wanted to make him rich, and came before him with gifts of vessels of silver and gold, as "they bring presents to a king" (Ps. 76:12) — but they did not succeed.

Eli Falk, one of the messengers, did so, and flatly contradicted his colleague who had gone with him on the community's errand to my father. But Joel Schwab, as a man of truth, stood by his words: he had changed nothing; he had done what they commanded him and kept to the words they had put in his mouth, without turning to the right or the left; he had added nothing and taken nothing away, good or bad. For this they fined Joel Schwab six hundred Reichstaler, which he had to pay at once into the community chest; and he did so gladly. Besides this they gave him a bitter punishment: not only was he put out of the community chamber, it was decreed that he be barred from any office and never again come to any matter of holiness all his days, and that he sit in the synagogue with the mourners and the excommunicated. So he sat as a mourner and an excommunicate for several years. The leader bore it all and accepted it for the love of truth; and all the days of R. Ber Kohen's life Joel Schwab never again came to any office in community affairs. The story is long; it remained in writing with R. Joel Schwab and was kept in his archive as a memorial.
