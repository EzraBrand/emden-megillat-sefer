"He has chastened me sore, but He has not given me over to death" (Ps. 118:18); He did not forsake me to be
destroyed, God forbid, "as a father the son in whom he delights" (Prov. 3:12); "He set my feet upon a rock"
(Ps. 40:3). "When I said, My foot slips, Your mercy, O Lord, held me up" (Ps. 94:18); "the right hand of the Lord is
exalted" (Ps. 118:16) — it supported and upheld me. So I said: I will declare Your name, O Lord, to my brothers and to
my descendants, so as not to forget His kindnesses — "forget not, my soul, all His benefits" (Ps. 103:2) — and "among
the many I will praise Him, for He stands at the right hand of the needy, to save him from those who judge his soul"
(Ps. 109:30–31). The greatness of the wonders that He did for me, the poorest of my clan (Judg. 6:15), I will make
known to the last generation. "Come and hear, all you who fear God, what He has done for my soul" (Ps. 66:16), "my life
is continually in my hand" (Ps. 119:109); "my mouth shall praise You with joyful lips" (Ps. 63:6), and with my pen I
will pour out His great kindness and make thanksgiving heard aloud, "that the generation to come might know, the
children yet to be born, who shall arise and tell their children" (Ps. 78:6), and give thanks to the Lord, for He is
good, for His kindness endures for ever; "for He has delivered the soul of the needy from the hand of evildoers"
(Jer. 20:13).

And this is the second reason: to strengthen weak hands, the crushed in spirit and the broken in heart, the insulted and
the persecuted who have no violence in their hands — as I am today. "Upon our necks we are persecuted" (Lam. 5:5) by
those who hate me without cause and repay evil for good; "they are my adversaries because I follow the good"
(Ps. 38:21). "Many shall see and fear, and shall trust in the Lord" (Ps. 40:4); "that they might set their hope in God,
and not forget the works of God" (Ps. 78:7); and their hearts shall not fail before the multitude of tyrants and the
hatred of the archers.

And the third: that the sun of my righteousness may shine, and no cloud rest on it, "from the wicked who despoil me, my
deadly enemies who compass me about" (Ps. 17:9), who spread an evil report about me to make me odious to the
inhabitants of the land (Gen. 34:30), to destroy me — a reproach in their hands — with their lies and their recklessness,
which spread in every direction through all the land, to their shame. Surely many of their libels will remain in the
world for some time. So necessity forced me to set out my affairs plainly before God and man, that my righteousness may
go forth as the light (Ps. 37:6); "He will deliver the innocent" (Job 22:30); and the truth is a witness for me. Let it
be to me "a covering of the eyes" (Gen. 20:16), and for my sons and descendants, may the Blessed One keep them.

I begin the story of my life, to make known the righteous acts of the Lord: that "those who wait for Him shall not be
ashamed" (Isa. 49:23), and none who take refuge in Him will be confounded for ever. "A very present help in trouble"
(Ps. 46:2), as I have seen with my own eyes many times. "I said in my haste, I am cut off" (Ps. 31:23) from the land of
the living; and I said, "Surely the darkness shall cover me" (Ps. 139:11), "the deep surrounded me" (Jon. 2:6), "and the
night shall be light about me"; "when I said, My foot slips, Your mercy, O Lord, held me up"; "though I sit in darkness,
the Lord is a light to me" (Mic. 7:8); "His anger is turned away and He comforts me" (Isa. 12:1); "He has put a new song
in my mouth, praise to our God" (Ps. 40:4). May the Lord put His word in my mouth and be with my lips and my pen, and may
His praise be made known to the generations to come.

of my birth he came home from the bath of Ems, where he had taken the cure of that spring between Passover and Shavuot.
He came to Altona after his wife, my mother the rebbetzin, of blessed memory, had given birth to me, on the fifteenth of
Sivan 5457 [1697] **[a]**. He circumcised me himself — after the despair, when the commandment had already been given to
another *mohel* — and brought me into the covenant with a great double joy: that he had returned to his health as before,
and that he had been given a son after despairing of one, for until then he had only daughters; and so he fulfilled the
commandment of circumcision with rejoicing.

I was the fourth child and the first son, tender and an only one to my father and my mother, after my mother had borne
three daughters. My parents trembled and quaked over me greatly, and I was brought up on their knees as a darling child,
in great pleasure, tenderness and longing. With all this my father hurried to put me into school when I turned three, so
that in my fifth year I had already finished learning the tractate *Berakhot* ⟨S: a word, not read⟩. I was so diligent at
the book that my companions of my own age ran after me and had not yet learned to read the prayers at that time. But
afterwards he held back from putting my brothers, born after me, into the teacher's house while they were such tender
children, for he said that it had weakened me.

If I set out to tell all that passed over me in the years of my childhood, the story would be very long, besides what is
hidden and forgotten from me. I will mention only a few grains: three terrible things that happened to me in the days of
my tender childhood. First, a sore broke out on my face, in the flesh only, and the sore spread from one side to the
other, so that I was in great danger, almost beyond hope; and the Lord "sent His word and healed" me (Ps. 107:20), without
leaving any mark of injury on my face, and no scar at all. Second, a leprous boil rose on my member and closed the opening
of the urine for some time, so that it caused my parents worry, grief and much sighing over me. Third, a heavy boil covered
my whole leg, like the boil of Job, with ugly sufferings.

From all these the Lord saved me — besides other evil and lasting illnesses that come to all children, such as smallpox
and measles, and the cutting of teeth. Above all I was very prone to swelling of the tonsils, to catarrh and cough, and to
burning of the urine, and sometimes to small blisters and red spots. And besides the blows "with which I was wounded in
the house of my friends" (Zech. 13:6) — the teachers into whose hands I was given to learn, who were for the most part
cruel and beat me without pity — and besides other pains and ailments that passed over me from my earliest years: "many
a time have they afflicted me from my youth, yet they have not prevailed against me" (Ps. 129:2) ⟨S: two words, perhaps
"to destroy …"⟩ — from the falling out of teeth and the growing of others in their place, from the pressing of the
fingernails and the like, they are too many to number.

[When my father was received] as rabbi of the Ashkenazi community of Amsterdam, my exile and my wandering began. My
father's household set out at the end of the winter of 5470 [1710]. It was the time of the thaw, after very heavy snows
in that fierce and mighty winter, and we were in great danger on the road: the wagon in which all the women and children
sat almost broke and fell into the waters that overflowed it, and "the waters came even to the soul" (Jon. 2:6). Our cry
went up on high; "had it not been the Lord who was on our side, then the waters had overwhelmed us, the stream had gone
over our soul" (Ps. 124:2–4). We came alive and in peace to another land, the country of Holland, which we had not known
before: new air, the customs of a new world. Many changes passed over us until we grew used to the nature of the
country, for the food and drink did not suit us according to our habit.

And above all, the quiet did not last; "this is the rest, give rest to the weary" (Isa. 28:12) — that they did not
allow. For at once the quarrels and persecutions began of the two factions of the strong men ⟨S: corrected to "of the
cantors"⟩, told above, each of which wanted to swallow the other alive. My father was pressed between them. Since he
could not reconcile them, and it was impossible to find both in the right, and since he protected the weak side so that
it should not be trampled, the opposing side declared war on him; "they embittered him, and shot at him, the archers";
they thought to bring his honour down to the ground, as said. And the Lord helped him and was a shield for him, and at
that time the congregation of the Sephardim supported him.

With all this it was "a time of trouble for Jacob" and confusion, from my father's suffering; besides which this
controversy caused much neglect of Torah. At that time I became bar mitzvah: "the twilight that I longed for He has turned
into trembling for me" (Isa. 21:4) **[b]** — "distress and darkness, the gloom of anguish" (Isa. 8:22), a time of worry,
grief and sighing. The times did not let me be diligent at the book and acquire perfection, for great lack of a companion,
a leader and a guide to deal with me in blessing. And there was no remedy for my lack of quiet, until the sins of that
place brought it about, and there came there the hypocritical serpent

In the middle of the winter of 5474 [1714] he travelled to London for some weeks, and sent us, his household, by ship

to Torah and to lineage. But for a reason known in the family my father would not agree and call the match good.

It was a great trial for me too, for I was already of understanding, and knew how to weigh things in the scales of right
judgement. It was not hidden from me that it was a very fitting match, the like of which was not to be found in the land
in every respect — in capital and wealth, and as a daughter of a good family — so that it looked to me like a very
straight road, by which I could easily reach the goal of perfection. I knew well that my father could give me nothing,
all the more since he could barely provide for himself and his household, moving about without knowing where he was
going; and the leader and his household loved me very much and truly pressed me to carry out their plan and gain their

up so fitting a thing. That blemish in the family was not a reason to reject it either, for it was not disqualifying in

will write with God's help. And that important woman mentioned above, who had longed for me and did not succeed, was still

When this trouble had passed, I saw a new world, a new nature and new customs. In my father-in-law's house, too, the
living was hard at that time, and I had to make do with the barest necessities, against the tenderness of my nature and
the habits I had learned in my father's house. And at once I tasted a taste more bitter than death, for my wife, of
blessed memory, was hard and stubborn, though she was gentle in speech and lovely; only my sins did this to me. Besides,
"when the barley is finished from the jar, strife comes knocking at the house" **[a]** in my father-in-law's house; and
they "ploughed with my heifer to find out my riddle" (Judg. 14:18) — the place where I had hidden my purse, the little
money I had received. I did not agree to give an outlet for the money and throw coins away for nothing; and they made
quarrels with me and almost separated me from my wife, so that I had no rest, no quiet and no peace.

Never in my life did I sit one day in tranquillity. When the flower of my youth blossomed, "from the womb of the morning,
the dew of my youth" (Ps. 110:3), the days of my boyhood and youth passed in poverty; all the times came in sorrow,
trouble and suffering; "from within and without my sighs are many" (Lam. 1:22), and all my joy was darkened (Isa. 24:11).
With all this, "the precepts of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart" (Ps. 19:9), the broken and contrite heart; they
were to me the joy of my heart and the restoring of my soul: "unless Your law had been my delight, I should then have
perished in my affliction" (Ps. 119:92). Only the main thing was missing: books. I had then only the four *Ṭurim*, which
I had brought with me, given me by my uncle ⟨S: a name, not read⟩, of blessed memory, as a wedding present. In them I
studied, as the good hand of the Lord was upon me, according to my understanding; besides them I did not have even a
volume of the Gemara as I wished, except on loan and with great difficulty. Though my father-in-law, of blessed memory,
had several sets of the Talmud, and his house was full of precious and honoured books, my eye had no access to them, and
I had no permission to take any of them to study as I needed.

With all this pressure and want, young men of desire at once set their eyes on me: scholars married and unmarried longed
to study with me. I wanted more to learn than to teach; even so I could not make their word and their pressing void and
refuse them, God forbid, also because I said: "from my students more than from all" (b. Ta'anit 7a) — they will keep my
learning in my hands. So I bent my shoulder and became "a servant under tribute" (Gen. 49:15), serving the service of the
Lord with companions who listened to my voice. Young men of the people and important scholars gathered to me, and I
studied with them Gemara, Rashi and Tosafot, the *Ṭur Oraḥ Ḥayyim* with the *Beit Yosef*, Bible, halakhah and aggadah; my
words were sweet to those who heard them, and their company was pleasant and agreeable to me, good fellowship. They even
wanted to provide the needs of my household amply, for their fathers were rich merchants, notables of the land

I went on a long road on which I had never travelled before. I travelled alone with men of Poland, in hard wandering,

we came upon another wagon, of armed gentile traders, going ahead of us. When the riders saw that wagon going before us
they turned back at once, and no evil touched us. Blessed is the Rescuer!

I came, thank God, to the holy community of Lwów at the end of the summer of 5478 [1718], and found my father's household
