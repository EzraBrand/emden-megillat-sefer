"the bed would be too short to stretch oneself" (Isa. 28:20); for it would need a book of its own, and even so I would not

Afterwards, in the year after my third marriage, as said, I went to great expense to have new square letters made for me in
Amsterdam, with vowel points and accents, which came to a large account, besides the expenses I had already had for tools and
new Rashi letters, which together came to a large sum. And now I began the work of the Siddur, in the year [5]505 [1745], and
I left other works, written and lying with me, and set my heart to take upon myself the yoke of the burden of this holy work,
for it is great; especially as in this work I had not yet written anything of the service for the work of the Lord, and
nothing had been written before at that time but the work *Luaḥ Eresh*, and that I left aside, so as not to provoke war with a
man who is not of my craft; I made for it only markers and a record of references **[a]** in the work of the Siddur, which
I called *Ammudei Shamayim*.

In it I included laws and customs that must be known for the whole
year; also the order of the services that were practised in the time of the Temple, and all the events that befell our
fathers, and the prophecies — on which day the prophets prophesied. All that is found in the Written and the Oral Torah that
depends on time, I set in its place and on its day, for all the months of the year, and even hours for all the days of the
week, fit for practice on the day itself, and the custom by night, on weekday and on holy day. I set everything apart by itself (cf. Gen.
21:29); I did not mix the matter of one day with another, for all the days of the year. All the prayers and the blessings, and the
texts of the early supplications, I explained with God's help, and I explained them according to the four senses (*PaRDeS*),
and customs, as far as I could bear. As a first undertaking it was a great, long and broad work, very heavy in quantity and
quality, for I took upon myself even bringing the words of all the blessings in number, and to make for them "buds and blossoms"
(cf. Ex. 25:33) in beautiful allusions. So too I set out in this work many plain senses, pleasant, sweet and lucid, both in
difficult verses of Scripture, beyond the plain way, and in astonishing and strange sayings of the sages, of blessed memory,
which became near and acceptable to the upright mind, with God's help.

And behold, at first a name went out at once for this work, and it found favour in the eyes of all who saw it, until whoever
saw it, "while it was yet in his hand he swallowed it" (Isa. 28:4); and almost as soon as the first part of it was finished,
they already longed to buy it. And it was this that troubled me. For through this zealots fell upon me, "a brood of sinful
men" (Num. 32:14), who "laid wanton charges against me" (Deut. 22:14); for they searched and found that I had written, in the
laws of conduct and of commerce, what the sages, of blessed memory, said in the Mishnah, that a man should not teach
his son to be a shopkeeper; and I had stealthily added some words in my own words: "And woe [to the money-changers] in these
generations" **[a]**. By this they were filled with fury and wrath, and the anger of the money-changers, brazen-faced, [was such that] they almost
thought to swallow me for nothing. They raged so, and said with full mouth, as I heard from behind truthful men, that if they
met me they would lay hands on me; and "blessed be the Lord, who delivered the soul of the needy from the hand of evildoers"
(Jer. 20:13)!

Indeed, these "sinners against their own souls" (Num. 17:3) bound themselves in a conspiracy of the wicked against me, and
came as one man to the chamber of the community by day, and roused the parnassim with outcry and great commotion, saying
that I was bringing them into danger. They spoke much evil of me, and asked them to command that the printed book be taken
from my hand and the page torn out of it; and to cover the nakedness of their shame with the garment of traitors, "the proud
forged a lie against me" (Ps. 119:69), that many things were found in this book that cause danger to all Jewry. They also
complained about two of the readings of the prayer texts in it. All this they did to multiply the shrill chatter against me;
and the parnassim acceded to them, to seat R. Y. Ḥ. and the court to look into this book of mine, whether they could find a
pretext against me.
