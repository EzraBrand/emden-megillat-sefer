# Jacob Emden, *Megillat Sefer* — pilot translation, pp. 3–8

Base text: ed. David Kahana, Warsaw 1896/97 (HebrewBooks 37017, Gershom Scholem's copy). Hebrew checked
against the scan (`data/text/sNNN.he.txt`). Translation by Claude (Opus 5.5), 20-Nov-2026. Draft, not reviewed.

Conventions:
- **[a]**, **[b]** … = Kahana's footnotes, translated at the end of each page.
- **⟨S: …⟩** = a handwritten reading in the margin of Scholem's copy, placed where its mark points. A key sheet
  in the copy (seen in the NLI scan) says that the margin readings follow the Oxford manuscript, that V marks an
  omission and X a substitution, and that the collation was made by Ḥayyim Wirszubski. Readings marked (?) are
  uncertain. Kahana's printed text is translated as printed; the margin readings are not merged into it.
- Biblical phrases that Emden weaves into his prose are rendered in the wording of the verse, with the
  reference in parentheses at the first strong allusion only.

"O Lord, You are my God; I will exalt You, I will praise Your name, for You have done wonders, counsels from
afar" (Isa. 25:1) **[c]**. The Lord has appeared to me through His deeds and the acts that He showed me
against my adversaries. "Oh that my words were now written, oh that they were inscribed in a book, … that
they were engraved in the rock for ever" (Job 19:23–24), in the writing of the house of Israel, so that
"children yet to be born may arise and tell them to their children" (Ps. 78:6) at the last day. "Then will
our mouth be filled with laughter and our tongue with song" (Ps. 126:2), for wickedness and iniquity will be
no more.

I will begin with my grandfather, my father's father, of blessed memory, whose name I bear: the gaon
R. Jacob son of R. Benjamin — may his merit shield me — an Ashkenazi of the stock of *Z"K* **[d]**. So they
used to sign their family name, for they were *zera kodesh*, "holy seed": tested and refined men in the days
of the persecutions and forced conversions, who remained faithful to the Lord for many generations, of the
greatest of the early Ashkenazi pietists.

My grandfather's father, mentioned above **[e]**, was one of the distinguished scholars of Vilna in those
days, before the decree of Chmiel, and he was a disciple of the gaon R. Jacob, head of the court in Lublin,
father of the gaon R. Heschel of blessed memory. He served him in the yeshiva, and R. Jacob gave him his
daughter as a wife **[f]**.

My grandfather, with his wife my grandmother(?), after many wanderings had passed over them, came to Altona
and saw their beloved son, my father and master(?), of blessed memory, in honour, after he had already
married my mother, the rebbetzin, of blessed memory, as told above, with the help of Heaven. From there they

A year later the wife of my grandfather's youth died. He was already an old man of more than seventy, and
the sages who were there compelled him to take a wife in his old age, for in the holy city a man is not
allowed to live without a wife, even if he is old and full of days. The Holy One, blessed be He, provided
him there with a fitting second match, also a woman of a priestly family, of a family of distinguished
lineage in Israel: she was the learned and outstanding old woman who had been there(?) before, and her name
was Judith, daughter of the famous gaon R. Naftali who was in Lublin — the grandfather of my aged
father-in-law, R. Naftali Katz, head of the court in Frankfurt am Main.

My grandfather R. Jacob also died in Jerusalem, at a good old age, seventy-three years old, and it was
written from there to my father and master, of blessed memory. At the time of the righteous man's death God
answered the Holy Land, and a great miracle happened. For the heavens had held back their waters; so they put with him in his grave a piece of cloth soaked in water, to make known their distress,
that they needed water — so that he would plead for mercy for the living, that they might not die of thirst.
